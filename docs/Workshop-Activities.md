# Workshop Activities

The workshop is designed around active participation rather than instructor-led demonstrations.

Participants work with their own professional context throughout the learning process, gradually building practical AI capability through guided exploration, discussion, experimentation, and reflection.

Every activity contributes to a tangible workplace outcome.

---

# Learning Journey

The workshop follows a structured sequence that mirrors how organisations successfully adopt AI.

```text
Work Task

↓

Opportunity

↓

AI Tool

↓

Prompt

↓

Evaluation

↓

Workflow

↓

Adoption
```

Each stage builds on the previous one, allowing participants to develop confidence through practice rather than observation.

---

# Example Learning Activities

## 1. Identify Work Tasks

Participants begin by analysing the tasks that make up their own role.

Using the task-level report, they identify activities that are repetitive, information-intensive, judgement-based, or highly collaborative.

The objective is to understand the work before introducing AI.

---

## 2. Explore AI Opportunities

Working individually and in small groups, participants discuss where AI may:

- automate routine activities
- augment professional judgement
- improve quality or consistency
- support decision making
- require continued human control

The emphasis is on critical thinking rather than technology enthusiasm.

---

## 3. Design Prompts

Participants create prompts for authentic workplace tasks.

Rather than following fixed prompt templates, they experiment, compare outputs, refine instructions, and discuss why some prompts produce better results than others.

Prompting is learned through discovery.

---

## 4. Evaluate AI Outputs

Participants critically review AI-generated responses.

Discussion focuses on:

- accuracy
- completeness
- relevance
- potential risks
- organisational context
- human oversight

This reinforces responsible AI use and professional judgement.

---

## 5. Build AI-Supported Workflows

Participants combine the task analysis, AI outputs, and their own expertise to design practical AI-supported workflows.

These workflows identify:

- where AI contributes
- where human judgement is required
- verification points
- opportunities for continuous improvement

The result is a workflow that can be transferred directly to the workplace.

---

## 6. Develop an Adoption Plan

The final activity encourages participants to identify:

- one workflow they will implement
- expected benefits
- potential barriers
- support required
- next steps

This helps bridge the gap between workshop learning and workplace application.

---

# The Facilitator's Role

Throughout the workshop, the facilitator acts as a guide rather than a lecturer.

The role includes:

- asking questions that stimulate analysis
- encouraging discussion and reflection
- helping participants interpret task-level data
- supporting experimentation
- challenging assumptions
- connecting activities to real workplace practice

The objective is not to provide answers, but to help participants develop confidence in making informed decisions about AI within their own professional context.

---

# Workshop Deliverables

By the end of the workshop, participants leave with practical outputs they can continue using after the training.

Typical deliverables include:

- completed task analysis
- AI opportunity assessment
- prompt iterations
- evaluated AI outputs
- documented AI-supported workflow
- personal adoption plan

The workshop therefore produces more than learning—it produces practical resources that support continued AI adoption.
